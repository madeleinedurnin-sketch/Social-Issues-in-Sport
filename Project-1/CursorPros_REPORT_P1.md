## Introduction
The rapid visibility and growth of women’s basketball has completely changed the way female athletes are viewed. How they are viewed through fans, media and even by themselves through social media has changed. Two mainstream athletes have become the center of this shift and it is Caitlan Clark and Angel Reese. Their rise in popularity was not just because of their talent on the court, it was also the narrative of their actions, comments and more competitive moments are framed and interpreted online. Social media has become one of the spaces where fans share their opinions and their own narratives about sports and athletes. It has turned a normal competition into very public conversations that are often polarized and highly discussed.

The media plays a huge role in continuing the rivalry narrative and shaping that picture of Reese and Clark pitted against each other. Many times a normal competitive interaction is exaggerated and it pushes the idea of a “personal rivalry” more than the actual sport itself. Now the focus is shifted away from basketball and toward the comparison and the drama between the two athletes. When either athlete posts or speaks publicly the audiences are quick to react and interpret the interactions and connect that to the narrative of the rivalry. This further fuels the narrative across all social media platforms. Every comment and interaction gets picked apart by the content creators and sports media. They want to keep the narrative of tension and personal conflict. Through the audience perspective, we are able to see how fans help spread the narrative and not just consume it, they are helping create it. This case also explores the bigger question of if this is really helping the growth of women’s basketball or if the rivalry focus is taking attention away from the athletes performance.   

Understanding these dynamics is very important working in the sports industry. Rivalry narratives can and will directly impact athletes. This puts pressure on them to perform not only as a competitor but also as public figures in an ongoing media narrative. From a sports management perspective, this kind of coverage can increase engagement and views short term, but it also can create a toxic environment and puts the focus on the drama rather than the game over time. The media is a major role player because they frame athletes like Clark and Reese and this shapes the fan culture and online discourse. Because of the growing importance of digital sports consumption, our research question is: How does the social media coverage of Angel Reese and Caitlin Clark, specifically the framing and frequency of each other, influence audience perception of the ongoing rivalry.

# Data & Materials

`ARCC_INS_POST.xlsx`  
`ARCC_YT_COMMENT.xlsx`  
`ARCC_YT_VIDEO.xlsx` 

To better understand how the rivalry narrative and how it developed over time, the analysis was divided into two different time periods of pre-2023 and 2023-2024. The pre-2023 represents audience discussion before the rivalry became a super well known and common media story, while the 2023-2024 period captures the peak competition between Reese and Clark. This includes their transition into the WNBA. Separating the data into these 2 phases allows us to track how the audience discussion, rivalry narratives and media framing intensified over time.    

The dataset that is required was extensive preprocessing before the analysis. To ensure more accurate keyword tracking the text data was standardized to improve consistency across platforms. To improve the quality of the dataset, spam comments, repetitive bot behavior, and empty text entries were filtered out. The analysis also included unique word and keyword frequency calculations. They were used to identify recurring themes, cross-athlete references  and recurring themes throughout the comment sections.   

This study relies on the ARRCC dataset and because it directly captures both athlete related content and audience reactions across major social media platforms. To be more specific, the project uses `ARCC_INS_COMMENT.xlsx` and `ARCC_INS_POST.xlsx` to analyze the audience discourse and visual framing on instagram. To evaluate Youtube comments, video titles and views and comment counts we used the `ARCC_YT_COMMENT.xlsx` and `ARCC_YT_VIDEO.xlsx`. To have a  more complete understanding of how the rivalry narratives are created, circulated and reinforced online we had to use both Instagram and Youtube.   

The overall dataset spans from 2017 through 2024, this allows for longitudinal analysis of how the Clark and Reese rivalry evolved from their collegiate career into their highly publicized transition to professional basketball. The timeline was intentionally divided  into “ Pre-2023” and “2023-2024”. The phases were to compare how audience discussion changed once the rivalry became a dominant subject to the media. During the cleaning process, the datasets were refined by removing spam-like comments and managing empty strings and also standardizing text for more accurate keyword analysis. By comparing audience comment trends with youtube engagement metrics like video views and interaction levels we know that additional attention was given to clickbait and engagement driven dynamics.   

## Analytical Approach & Justification   
The analytical approach for project 1 was focused on turning large amounts of audience commentary into measurable data that directly answered our research question. To do this, the analysis used a multi-layered keyword frequency and comparative text analysis across our 4 major constructs: personality differences, post and mention frequency, skill comparison and beauty standards. The keywords were specifically chosen and categorized to identify how the audience discussed both athletes. For example the personality based terms included words like "cocky," “classy,” “humble,” and “arrogant,” and the skill bases comparison had terms like "goat,” “trash,” “stats,” and “points.”   

This operationalization strategy was important because this allowed us to use qualitative audience reactions and transform it into quantifiable data that could be compared across time periods and platforms. The cleaned data from Instagram and Youtube were analyzed through keyword matching and AI assisted natural language processing to track unique word frequencies, cross mentions, and recurring themes. Once the keyword frequencies were identified, the comments were grouped into their framing category. They were considered competitive framing, collaborative framing, media manufactured framing and neutral framing.   

To strengthen the analysis, the project compared the frequency of the rivalry related discourse between the pre-2023 and the 2023-2024 time period. To determine whether the increase in rivalry framing was significant statistically rather than random, we used the chi-square test of independence. Additional comparisons were made between skill based comments and appearance based comments. This helped us better understand how the audiences compared and evaluated Caitlan Clark and Angle Reese when they were in the same discussions. 

## Results
The results showed strong evidence that the media driven rivalry narrative has become more deeply embedded in the audience discussions online. The findings we had were represented through two main visualizations: bar charts for cross reference frequency, cross reference rates, framing in comments for both athletes, and sentiment distribution. One of the biggest findings was the major difference in cross reference rates between Instagram and Youtube. Youtube comments showed more rivalry focused discussions than Instagram. On Instagram only 3.26% mentioned Caitlin Clark and 4.71% mentioned Angel Reese. In comparison, Youtube had a much higher cross reference rate, with 21.6% mentioning Clark and 13.49% mentioning Reese. These findings suggest that Youtube discourse is way more centered around comparison and rivalry than the Instagram discourse.   

Sentiment analysis showed that despite the high levels of competitive and dismissive framing, the overall emotional tone of the cross reference comments were slightly positive across both of the platforms. Instagram comments mentioning Clark had a mean Vader score of 0.287, while Reese mentions averaged 0.322. On Youtube, sentiment scores were lower but still slightly positive. Clark mentions averaged 0.142 and Reese mentions averaging 0.134. These results tell us that rivalry and appreciation can exist simultaneously within audience discussions.   

Overall, the results demonstrate that both the frequency of cross athlete mentions and the way they are framed strongly contribute to how audiences perceive and reinforce the rivalry between Reese and Clark. 

## Interpretation & Discussion

The findings from this study suggest to us that the audiences have fully bought what the media is selling. They have bought into the media created rivalry between Reese and Clark. The high amount of cross mentions throughout the Instagram and Youtube datasets showed that the fans rarely discuss one athlete without bringing up the other. Even their posts that are focused on personal accomplishments often shifted back to comparison and rivalry, showing how dominant this narrative has become on platforms.   

These findings directly support Media Framing Theory because they demonstrate how repetitive media coverage shapes audiences' interpretation over time. Sports media and the digital creators frequently frame the interactions between Reese and Clark through conflict and comparison. This causes audiences to expect rivalry driven drama whenever the athletes get mentioned together. The results show that fans actively participate in shaping these narratives through comment sections, online engagement, and reposts that reinforces rivalry focused content across the platforms.  

Another major takeaway was that much of the audience discussion extended on basketball itself. Many comments focused on personality based framing and using the terms “cocky,” “classy,” and “arrogant,” suggesting that audiences often judged the athletes based on personality and their public image rather than their talent or athletic performance on the court. This is important because this shows how female athletes are still heavily under the microscope and evaluated through their social interactions connected to behavior, confidence and appearance. In a lot of cases it became less about basketball and more about what they symbolized culturally to different groups of fans.   

The platform differences with the data were significant. Youtube content tended to produce more polarized and aggressive comments compared to Instagram. Videos with click bait titles centered around conflict and controversy generated much higher levels of engagement and competitive cross mentions. This tells us that controversy based content performs especially well within Youtube. Instagram still contained rivalry framing but discussions there appeared to be more connected to the athletes themselves rather than purely the drama. These platform differences demonstrate how algorithms and content structures influence the way fans and audiences engage with sports online.   

The implications of these findings are important for sports organizations, leagues and media. Rivalry narratives can be extremely effective for generating attention and increasing the engagement around a sport. The Clark and Reese rivalry has clearly helped drive conversations, media coverage, and visibility for women's basketball. However, there is a long term risk associated with heavily relying on conflict. Constantly framing women's basketball around rivalry may create a toxic fan base. This can have environments where audiences focus more on attacking athletes themselves and debating rather than appreciating the sport. Additionally, the athletes themselves face this pressure of constantly being compared against each other and can overshadow their individual accomplishments and put an emotional strain on them as a public figure.   

Overall, the results show that modern sports rivalries are no longer shaped only through athletic competition and performance. Instead, they are continuously being built from media framing, audience participation, and engagement driven culture of social media. The rivalry between Reese and Clark highlights how media audiences actively help create sports narratives and how these narratives can quickly become larger than the sport. 

## Ethical Reflection & Limitations
Although the findings provide very meaningful insight into digital sports, several limitations need to be acknowledged. Social media environments are heavily influenced by spam accounts, bots, algorithms, and users that intentionally post extreme opinions for views. Some inauthentic and or exaggerated commentary may still remain within the dataset even with data cleaning measures in place. To add, because the rivalry became very visible these last few years, the pre-2023 data is more limited compared to the huge increase in content during 2023-2024.  

We utilized Antigravity and Cursor to help organize processes and analyze the large datasets throughout this project. While the platform helped identify patterns and sort patterns within the datasets. There are still limitations associated with AI assisted and software driven analysis, especially regarding interpretation, keyword recognition, and contextual nuance within the comments.   

There were also important ethical considerations throughout the research, no usernames or identified people were included on this project. Furthermore, analyzing comments related to beauty standards and personality traits needed careful framing to avoid reinforcing the harmful stereotypes being studied. The goal of this project is not to validate toxic comments but to critically examine how these narratives are created and reinforced within the digital sports culture. Finally, we recognized the importance of us remaining as objective as possible throughout the analysis, despite possible personal or institutional connections to the athletes involved.
