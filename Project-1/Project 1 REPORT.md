# **Introduction**

The rapid visibility and growth of women’s basketball has completely changed the way female athletes are viewed. How they are viewed through fans, media and even by themselves through social media has changed. Two mainstream athletes have become the center of this shift and it is Caitlan Clark and Angel Reese. Their rise in popularity was not just because of their talent on the court, it was also the narrative of their actions, comments and more competitive moments are framed and interpreted online. Social media has become one of the spaces where fans share their opinions and their own narratives about sports and athletes. It has turned a normal competition into very public conversations that are often polarized and highly discussed.   
The media plays a huge role in continuing the rivalry narrative and shaping that picture of Reese and Clark pitted against each other. Many times a normal competitive interaction is exaggerated and it pushes the idea of a “personal rivalry” more than the actual sport itself. Now the focus is shifted away from basketball and toward the comparison and the drama between the two athletes. When either athlete posts or speaks publicly the audiences are quick to react and interpret the interactions and connect that to the narrative of the rivalry. This further fuels the narrative across all social media platforms. Every comment and interaction gets picked apart by the content creators and sports media. They want to keep the narrative of tension and personal conflict. Through the audience perspective, we are able to see how fans help spread the narrative and not just consume it, they are helping create it. This case also explores the bigger question of if this is really helping the growth of women’s basketball or if the rivalry focus is taking attention away from the athletes performance.   
Understanding these dynamics is very important working in the sports industry. Rivalry narratives can and will directly impact athletes. This puts pressure on them to perform not only as a competitor but also as public figures in an ongoing media narrative. From a sports management perspective, this kind of coverage can increase engagement and views short term, but it also can create a toxic environment and puts the focus on the drama rather than the game over time. The media is a major role player because they frame athletes like Clark and Reese and this shapes the fan culture and online discourse. Because of the growing importance of digital sports consumption, our research question is: How does the social media coverage of Angel Reese and Caitlin Clark, specifically the framing and frequency of each other, influence audience perception of the ongoing rivalry. 

# **Related Literature**

	This project connects to other course concepts that involve media framing, narrative construction and the social media construction of female athletes online. It examines how audiences participate in the building and reinforces the narratives through the interpretation of athletes through social media and the sports media. This study gives us a better picture of how comment sections like Instagram and Youtube create different cultures that help strengthen or push forward the rivalry narrative. The analysis uses Media Framing Theory, this explains how we present content, selective emphasis, and repetition can shape the way audiences interpret events and people. This project also incorporates ideas related to audience co-creation. This shows that fans are not simply passive consumers of sports media, but they are active participants who help build and push the “rivalry” narrative on these platforms.   
	While past research has heavily focused on how female athletes are framed through stereotypes tied to their appearance, being a female, and their teamwork, there is still limited research on digital media creating and reinforcing intense rivalry narratives between two female athletes over time. The data used in this study gives good insight into how references between athletes are interpreted, amplified and reshaped by highly engaged online audiences across the media.   
This project strongly connects to the broader concepts surrounding narrative construction, media presentation and the changing social view of female athletes on digital spaces. For us to better understand how the rivalry between Angel Reese and Caitlin Clark operates online, we apply the Media Framing Theory to examine how the audiences are influenced by media's repeated narratives. Sports media is really good about isolating short on court clips, quotes and gestures and presenting them in a way that encourages the audience to interpret the interactions with animosity. Framing theory helps explain how a simple moment or comment can quickly move into something large scale like a media controversy. It will be fueled by repetition, online engagement and the audience participation.   
Furthermore, this research is grounded in the theories of audience co-creation and participatory culture. In the past, sports media mainly operated as a one way system. This was where networks controlled the storyline and the audience just consumed it. Today, platforms like Instagram and Youtube allow fans to actively discuss and influence how these athletes and rivalries are perceived on social platforms. Comment sections have been more than simple reactions and they have been used more for public spaces where fans can debate and reinforce and spread the narratives. As audiences continue engaging with content that pits Reese and Clark against each other, algorithms are encouraged to push even more content that involves the drama. This creates a cycle where rivalry and drama become highly marketable forms of engagement.    
There is still limited research on how aggressive and highly individualized rivalry between female athletes are created and sustained online. Previous sports sociology research has widely explored how female athletes are framed through femininity, appearance, or teamwork rather than their athletic dominance in their sport. Iconic sports rivalries like Magic Johnson v Larry Bird were the narrative primarily associated with male athletes. The Reese and Clark dynamic represents a major shift in how women’s sports are consumed and commercialized within the media spaces. This project helps address the gap by providing detailed insight into how athletes, media and fans all together shape rivalry narratives online. Analyzing how the athletes reference one another and how the audience respond to that, this research highlights how modern day rivalries are socially constructed and continuously pushed through digital interactions. Ultimately seeing how audiences contribute to broader understanding of female athlete branding in today's sports culture.
